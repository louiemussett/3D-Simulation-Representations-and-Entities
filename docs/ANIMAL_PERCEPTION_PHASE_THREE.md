# Animal perception Phase 3 — entity-specific proximity

Proximity is now evaluated from each participant's own perspective. The same
pair can therefore produce high avoidance for one animal and high tolerance or
attraction for the other.

Each directional relationship evaluates continuous attraction, tolerance and
avoidance curves from:

- physical distance and body-contact span;
- individual space and intrusion-tolerance traits;
- observed affinity, grievance, kinship and group membership;
- reproductive compatibility;
- fear and condition;
- learned familiarity, trust and threat expectation;
- the observer's evidence-based predator-intent estimate.

Predator intent enters only through the existing observer inference. The model
does not read a predator's private target or decision state. A threatening
approach can therefore increase avoidance, while ambiguous proximity remains
ambiguous.

Close encounters update bounded per-entity relationship records. Friendly and
tolerated outcomes gradually build trust; warnings, retreats and attacks build
threat expectation. Familiarity is not automatically friendship, and the two
directions of a relationship are learned independently.

Moving and decision logic continue to consume the established encounter kinds
(`affiliate`, `ignore`, `orient`, `warn`, `retreat`, `attack` and group rally
responses), preserving compatibility while replacing their scalar trigger with
the richer directional pressure model.
