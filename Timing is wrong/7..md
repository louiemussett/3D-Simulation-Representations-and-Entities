Yes — **very likely.** The calendar structure strongly suggests that the 120-day year comes from treating each of the four seasons as a single 30-day “ecological month”:

- 1 ecological month = **30 days**
    
- 4 seasons × 30 days = **120 days**
    
- therefore 1 ecological year = **120 days**
    

The clock module explicitly defines both `DAYS_PER_ECOLOGICAL_MONTH = 30` and `DAYS_PER_ECOLOGICAL_YEAR = 120`.

So effectively the simulation currently behaves like:

**Spring — 30 days → Summer — 30 days → Autumn — 30 days → Winter — 30 days → next year**

rather than the real-world approximation:

**Spring — ~91 days → Summer — ~92 days → Autumn — ~91 days → Winter — ~90 days → 365-day year.**

If your intention was for the simulation to model a reasonably Earth-like annual cycle, then **120 days is probably an accidental consequence of compressing each season to one 30-day month**, rather than something you'd necessarily want biologically.

And this matters quite a lot. Your animals' maturation ages, gestation periods and lifespans are expressed in days. So a `maxAge: 420` animal currently lives for **3.5 ecological years**, not 420/365 ≈ **1.15 years**. Likewise, an 80-day maturation age is **two-thirds of an entire current year**.

I would seriously consider changing the ecological year to **365 days while keeping the 3-real-hour-per-year observation target unchanged**. That would preserve the player's pacing while making seasons, gestation, maturity and lifespan much easier to interpret biologically.
