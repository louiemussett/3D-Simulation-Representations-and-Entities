## The important distinction is “menstrual cycle” versus “estrous cycle”

For most of your animals, **menstrual cycle** isn't the technically correct term.

Humans and a limited number of other mammals menstruate.

Most mammals—including deer, wolves, foxes, cats and ungulates—have an **estrous cycle**.

The useful simulation variable is therefore something like:

> **female reproductive cycle / estrous recurrence**

rather than human-style menstruation.

And some species are:

- polyestrous: cycle repeatedly;
    
- seasonally polyestrous: repeat cycles only during a breeding season;
    
- monoestrous: normally one reproductive cycle per year;
    
- induced ovulators: ovulation can be triggered by mating.
    

Your single 28/36-day system currently collapses all of these into one mechanism.

## So the best biological fix is not “scale gestation by 3”

It is:

> **make gestation and female reproductive cycling species-specific, based on the real analogue.**

The 365-day year then gives those biological processes the correct annual context.

For example:

|Sim species|Approx. analogue|Current cycle|Current gestation|Better reproductive structure|
|---|---|--:|--:|---|
|Valley Grazer|deer|28 d|60 d|~28 d return-to-estrus, ~198 d gestation, seasonal|
|Ridge Hunter|wolf|36 d|90 d|annual estrus, ~60–63 d gestation|
|Brush Fox|fox|36 d|38 d|annual/strongly seasonal breeding, ~50–55 d gestation|
|Shadow Stalker|medium cat|36 d|82 d|~25–35 d cycle, ~80–100 d gestation|
|Woodland Browser|deer-like|36 d|68 d|~20–30 d cycle, ~190–210 d gestation|

That would be far more biologically coherent.

## Why the 365-day year then becomes important

With a 120-day year, a realistic 198-day deer gestation would span:

**1.65 years**

which is absurd in seasonal terms.

With a 365-day year, it occupies:

**0.54 years**

which is correct.

That means once you decide that the **estrous cycle and gestation should be literal biological day values**, the 120-day year becomes fundamentally incompatible with the biology.

That's the decisive point.

A deer-like female needs to be able to:

> breed in autumn → gestate for ~198 days → give birth in spring.

You simply cannot model that properly inside a 120-day annual cycle while also treating 28-day estrous cycles as literal days.

So I would now say the strongest biological architecture is:

**365-day year + species-specific estrous biology + gestation in real biological days.**

That is more important than immediately fixing lifespan or even maturity.

If you wanted to prioritise changes, I would do them in this order:

1. **365-day year**
    
2. **species-specific reproductive cycle**
    
3. **species-specific gestation**
    
4. seasonal breeding windows
    
5. dependency/weaning
    
6. sexual maturity
    
7. lifespan
    

The first three together would immediately make pregnancy and reproduction much more biologically coherent.

And importantly, it means **do not scale the 28-day Grazer cycle to ~85 days** when you change the calendar. Keep it around 28 days and instead move its gestation from 60 toward roughly 190–200 days if deer is the intended analogue.
